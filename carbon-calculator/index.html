    Methodology & sources

    Emission factors are rough, commonly-cited public averages (comparable to typical DEFRA/EPA-style consumer calculators) for travel (car by type, bus, rail, flights), home heating (natural gas), and diet (by broad dietary pattern). They are not lifecycle-audited or a substitute for a professional carbon audit — treat the total as a rough order of magnitude and the category breakdown as a guide to where your biggest levers are.

    Electricity emissions do use a country-specific factor: once you pick a country, your grid's actual carbon intensity (grams CO₂ per kWh) is used instead of a flat global average, from Our World in Data / Ember. This varies enormously — from under 30 gCO₂/kWh in hydro/nuclear-heavy grids like Norway or France to over 600 gCO₂/kWh in coal-heavy grids — so this one factor matters a lot for accuracy.

    The "Shopping & Goods" category is the roughest part of this calculator: it maps a subjective consumption level to a broad kg CO₂e/year range in the spirit of environmentally-extended input-output (EEIO) lifestyle-footprint studies (e.g. Ivanova et al. 2016), not a bottom-up calculation. Treat it as a directional nudge, not a number to optimize precisely.

    The national comparison figure is each country's most recent per-capita CO2 (excluding land use/forestry) from the World Bank — an all-sectors average (industry, transport, power generation, government), not a personal lifestyle figure, so it's context rather than a like-for-like comparison.
